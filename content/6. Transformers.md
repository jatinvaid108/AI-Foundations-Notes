# ⚡ **Transformers (Deep Learning for NLP)**

## 🔹 Background: Why Transformers?

Before Transformers, **RNNs** (Recurrent Neural Networks) were used for text/sequential data.

### **Problem with RNNs**

- RNNs process words **one by one** in order.
    
- **Struggle with long-range dependencies** — meaning they forget or give low importance to words far back in the sentence.
    

**Example:**

> “Jane threw the frisbee and the dog fetched it.”

- RNN has trouble remembering that **“it” refers to “frisbee”**, because it occurred many steps earlier



![[WhatsApp Image 2025-10-15 at 20.36.32_8b00b99a.jpg]]


## 🔹 How Transformers Help

Transformers solve this problem using **Attention Mechanism**:

### **Attention Mechanism**

- Instead of reading words one by one, it **looks at all words in the sentence simultaneously**.
    
- It decides **which words are important** for predicting the next word.
    

**Example with above sentence:**

- To predict “fetched,” the model attends to both **“dog”** and **“frisbee”**.
    
- It understands context without forgetting long-range dependencies.
    

✅ Key idea: **No more sequential bottleneck like RNNs**.

## 🔹 Key Components of a Transformer

|Component|Purpose|Simple Explanation|
|---|---|---|
|**Token & Tokenization**|Breaks text into smaller pieces (words or subwords)|“Jane threw the frisbee” → [“Jane”, “threw”, “the”, “frisbee”]|
|**Encoder**|Converts input tokens into **vector representations**|Each word becomes a **numerical vector** capturing meaning & context|
|**Decoder**|Generates output sequence word by word|Takes encoder vectors + previously generated words → predicts next word|

---

### 🔹 Number of Tokens

- Longer or more complex sentences → more tokens.
    
- Each token has a **vector representation**, which the model uses to understand meaning and relationships.
    

---

## 🔹 How Transformers Generate Text

1. **Input:** “Jane threw the frisbee and the dog…”
    
2. **Tokenization:** Break sentence into tokens → `[Jane, threw, the, frisbee, and, the, dog]`
    
3. **Encoder:** Converts tokens into vectors representing meaning/context.
    
4. **Attention:** Model decides which tokens to focus on for predicting the next word.
    
    - Example: When predicting “fetched,” it focuses on “dog” + “frisbee.”
        
5. **Decoder:** Uses vectors + attention → predicts next token (“fetched”)
    
6. **Repeat:** Continues token by token to generate complete text.
    

---

## 🔹 Summary (Simple Version)

- **RNNs:** Read sequentially → forget long-range info
    
- **Transformers:** Look at all words → pay attention to important words → handle long dependencies
    
- **Encoder:** Converts words → vectors
    
- **Decoder:** Generates next word → new sequence
    
- **Tokens:** Pieces of words/text used for computation
    
- **Attention:** Mechanism to focus on relevant words for context
    

---

✅ **Example in layman terms:**

> Input: “Jane threw the frisbee and the dog …”  
> Transformer sees all words, focuses on **“frisbee”** and **“dog”**, and predicts “fetched it” correctly.
